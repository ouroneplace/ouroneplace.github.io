## Original visitor outcomes
1. Request accessible electronic text at a city office and **test** that it actually works rather than assume a scan is accessible.
2. Request qualified sign-language interpreting for a complex public meeting and confirm participation; never presume captions or a family member will work for everyone.
3. Ask for an accessible alternative to a blocked online form, without sharing credentials and without assuming a deadline automatically changes.
4. Optional **local request-wording builder** with 3 explicitly selected dimensions: setting / effective communication method / verification check, readable preview, manually selectable text, stale-preview invalidation, and reset. No personal name, diagnosis, ID, URL, email address, passwords, files, storage, automatic third-party request, or outgoing message. Full static lessons and sample script remain available with JavaScript off. The builder produces preparation wording only; does not send a request or determine a legal right.

## Direct official sources reviewed Nov 13, 2026
- U.S. Department of Justice: https://www.ada.gov/topics/effective-communication/
- DOJ effective communication provisions and companion/interpreter caveats: https://www.ada.gov/resources/effective-communication/
- DOJ State/Local Government Title II overview: https://www.ada.gov/topics/title-ii/
These apply to U.S. public service settings and covered public accommodations, **not** all providers/countries or a universal guarantee. The relevant organization, communication need, aids, advance notice, effective alternatives, possible burden/alteration exceptions and whether the person chooses to involve a trusted adult may affect the right course. This page offers educational practice and is not legal advice or eligibility assurance. Avoid minor/family interpreter absolutes; DOJ guidance includes narrow exceptions. Refer to live officials/qualified disability-rights advice where access is disputed or urgent.
