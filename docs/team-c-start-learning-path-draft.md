## Original U.S. primary sources directly verified Nov 14, 2026 UTC

1. **Institute of Education Sciences (U.S. Department of Education), What Works Clearinghouse (2007), [Organizing Instruction and Study to Improve Student Learning](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1)**. Official original practice guide; it specifically recommends spacing practice over time, interleaving examples and problem attempts, combining verbal/visual descriptions, revisiting important content, and explanatory questioning. Although authored for educators and applicable to academic subjects, we use **limited generic principles** for a brief self-directed beginner step, not clinical cognitive evaluation or claims that every visitor will learn equally.
2. **Federal Trade Commission (May 2021), [Choosing a Vocational School or Certificate Program](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/choosing-vocational-school-or-certificate-program)**. Original guidance advises independently comparing programs, verifying accreditation/licensing recognition, written costs and outcomes, and not assuming a job guarantee. This only informs a fictional paid-training scam/pressure scenario; a person is not automatically guaranteed employment, licensing, transfer credits or a financial-aid offer. The public page does not recommend purchasing any program or handle tuition.

The separate U.S. Department of Education LINCS learner center was discovered by search, but automated direct access returned 403 in this review; **it is not linked as a verified directly opened source** or used to claim today's availability. Both linked original official sources above successfully opened; offsite links are opt-in `target="_blank"` and `rel="noopener noreferrer"`.

## Safety, language, access, and proofs

- Fictional text distinguishes a small learning step from a measured skill or credential; does not pretend to teach mathematics, certify professional learning, diagnose disabilities, or predict a job result. Always check for a resource's current accessibility, credentials and cost before putting sensitive data or money into it.
- Forms use native `fieldset`/`legend`, labeled radio/select controls, explicit feedback, accessible skip-to-focus target and keyboard operation, local example updated only through textContent, no persistent browser API, no automatic third-party source load and no sensitive-text entry.
- Static text, fictional options/answers and paper model are available with disabled JS and without payments. Print preview and 200%-root text subject to exact test results.
- Avoid promising that every outside learning resource has captions/audio/transcripts. The visitor chooses a format to *look for*, verifies fit, or asks the provider.
- Physical smartphone, native VoiceOver/TalkBack, representative human comprehension, every learner's accessibility needs and credentials program advice need proportional independent review where indicated. Synthetic Chromium cannot establish these.
- Every source/QA claim must be tagged with exact GitHub SHA, completed log and correct status. No early PASS claim. Team C must fix own source and focused test defects on the same draft branch before HQ intake.
