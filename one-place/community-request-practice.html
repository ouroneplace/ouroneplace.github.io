Ask, Check, Follow Up

If you see something in your community that could be improved, you can start with one clear question. Learn to find the appropriate channel, describe what you actually know and plan a safe next step.

Practice without sharing your details. The examples are fictional. Do not enter real names, addresses, phone numbers, medical details or sensitive reports here. This page does not contact a public office, send your message, save your writing or determine which agency is responsible.

Five steps for a community request

  1. Notice: Describe the observable situation. Separate what you saw from guesses about why it happened.
  2. Check: Find the relevant city, county, school, park or other official operator through its current official site or public contact channel. Responsibilities differ by place.
  3. Ask: Use a respectful, specific question or request. Avoid publishing another person's private information.
  4. Keep track: Note any reference number or instructions outside this practice page, somewhere you control. Do not assume a response time or outcome.
  5. Follow up or stop: Use the official follow-up route, seek trusted assistance, or reconsider the request if new facts change what is needed.

Safety comes before a form. Never enter an unsafe area to gather details, confront people, or upload anyone's private information. For immediate danger or a medical emergency, use local emergency services rather than this practice. A nonemergency question may not be handled by the first office you contact.

Open the complete paper example

Notice: Two public notices about a fictional center's Saturday hours disagree.

Request: “Could you confirm the current Saturday drop-in hours and where official updates are posted?”

Channel: Find the actual operator using an official city, county or center page; never trust an unsolicited message as the only source.

Follow-up: Follow that office's published instructions; if the accurate hours are available, no further request may be needed. Keep any private reference numbers outside this webpage.

Find the real local starting point

Which public office or community organization can answer depends on where you live and who runs the service. For U.S. locations, the USAGov state and local government directory (external) is one optional starting point, not a promise that a specific office or service is available. Outside the U.S., use the appropriate official local authority or service provider.
