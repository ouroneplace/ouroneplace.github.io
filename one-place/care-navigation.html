Care Navigation Guide

Helping yourself or another person move through healthcare? Choose a situation, follow three practical checks, and connect to the right existing One Place tool. No account or personal records needed.

Respect the person: Ask what help they want and who may receive their private information. If someone cannot make a decision, consent and legal representation rules vary by location. Avoid guessing about authority. This guide does not diagnose, change medicines, book care, replace a professional or determine legal rights.

Emergency: For immediate danger or a medical emergency, use your local emergency number (911 in the U.S.). For new or worsening concerns, follow clinical instructions and contact qualified care promptly. Local service availability and rules differ worldwide; the external sources below primarily explain U.S. care.

PATH 1 OF 4

Supporting someone's appointment

Help the person be heard, rather than speaking over them.

Why this matters

A support person can help with questions, access needs and remembering instructions, but the patient's wishes still matter.

What to do

Check the result

Can the person explain their preferred next step in their own words, with help if they want it?

When to stop or ask for help: Do not assume family status alone authorizes record access or medical decisions. Ask the provider about consent or legally appointed representation.

PATH 2 OF 4

Coming home after a hospital stay

Make the transition plan clear before the next handoff.

Why this matters

Discharge instructions can involve several providers, new appointments, medicines, home services and warning signs.

What to do

Check the result

Can the person or authorized support person identify who to call, what follow-ups are expected, and which warning signs need urgent care?

When to stop or ask for help: If the person is getting worse or shows concerning symptoms, use the discharge instructions and promptly contact the care team or emergency services as appropriate; do not wait for this checklist.

PATH 3 OF 4

Waiting for test results or a referral

Make follow-up responsibilities explicit; do not assume silence means normal.

Why this matters

A result or referral can be delayed or misunderstood unless the patient knows when, where and from whom to expect news.

What to do

Check the result

Can you name the responsible office, expected timing and next contact if nothing happens?

When to stop or ask for help: Do not interpret a test result or change treatment through this guide. For new or worsening symptoms, seek appropriate professional advice rather than waiting for paperwork.

PATH 4 OF 4

Removing a barrier to receiving care

Practical barriers deserve a specific, confirmed plan.

Why this matters

Transport, language, mobility, costs and scheduling needs can disrupt care even when a visit is medically important.

What to do

Check the result

Have you confirmed one realistic next step with the organization that can provide it?

When to stop or ask for help: Do not assume a service is free, available or covered. For urgent symptoms, seek timely professional care instead of delaying until logistical questions are solved.

  • National Institute on Aging: accompanying someone to an appointment (external) ↗
