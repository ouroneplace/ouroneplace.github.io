Know the document: EOB or bill?

Why it matters

An Explanation of Benefits (EOB) is a claim summary from an insurer. It is not a request for payment.

What to know

An EOB usually identifies the service, date, provider, billed amount, plan payment, and your possible responsibility. A provider sends a separate bill.

How to use the skill

  1. Look at the document heading and who sent it.
  2. Match the date and service to care you received.
  3. If you have questions, use the insurer or provider contact printed on the document.

Check the result

You can tell whether a document explains insurance processing or asks you to pay.

Common mistake: Treating an EOB as an invoice.

When to stop and ask: A medical statement you do not recognize or that contains someone else's details needs direct verification.

Compare a bill with the claim

Why it matters

Amounts can differ because a billed charge, allowed amount, insurer payment, and patient responsibility are different fields.

What to know

Before paying, verify the dates, provider, services, insurance adjustments, and payments you already made.

How to use the skill

  1. Find a claim or service date on the EOB and bill; compare the same service.
  2. Compare the insurer's stated patient share with the provider's billed balance, accounting for payments already made.
  3. If the balance does not make sense, ask the provider billing office and plan to reconcile it.

Check the result

You can identify one mismatch and which office you will ask about it.

Common mistake: Assuming the largest dollar figure is always the amount you owe.

When to stop and ask: If a document looks fraudulent or a payment demand is urgent, verify contacts independently rather than using an unverified link.

Find the cost-sharing terms

Why it matters

A low monthly premium does not tell the whole story about likely medical costs.

What to know

Premiums, deductibles, copays, coinsurance, coverage exclusions, and out-of-pocket limits have different roles. Your plan's own document controls.

How to use the skill

  1. Find the Summary of Benefits and Coverage or relevant plan document.
  2. Identify the deductible, fixed copays, percentage coinsurance, covered network, and out-of-pocket rules.
  3. Ask which services and providers are covered; do not assume every expense counts toward a limit.

Check the result

You can locate the deductible and ask whether a specific service is covered and in-network.

Common mistake: Believing every bill is covered once an out-of-pocket limit is reached.

When to stop and ask: If a service or eligibility rule is unclear, confirm with the plan before making costly non-emergency decisions.

Ask about an unexpected charge

Why it matters

A confusing bill can involve an error, missing insurance processing, a duplicate charge, or a cost that requires further explanation.

What to know

You can request an itemized bill, compare claim records, ask about a review or appeal, and inquire about financial help. Protections differ by insurance and location.

How to use the skill

  1. Request an itemized statement and record whom you contacted and when.
  2. Contact the provider and insurer to ask about discrepancies or an appeal process.
  3. If payment is difficult, ask about financial assistance and payment options; check current local protections and response deadlines.

Check the result

You can state the disputed item, the question, and the next contact without entering personal details here.

Common mistake: Ignoring a disputed bill indefinitely or believing a website can determine your legal responsibility.

When to stop and ask: Collection notices, appeal deadlines, or legal papers call for prompt, qualified assistance. Do not rely on this practice page to calculate a deadline.
