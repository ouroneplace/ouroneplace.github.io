Military Family Move Handoff Practice

A military move affects more than packing. When questions involve transportation, a child's school, health coverage, or ongoing family support, knowing who to contact first can prevent confusion. Rehearse four invented situations without entering orders, military identifiers, medical details, addresses or children's names.

Scope and safety: This exercise covers U.S. military-family relocation support, not VA benefit eligibility, moving orders, travel entitlements, school placement, health plan enrollment, EFMP eligibility or legal advice. Actual actions vary by branch, duty station, status and family circumstances. Follow your official orders and current agency guidance; confirm details with the responsible office. We do not collect or retain personal information.

A four-step handoff that avoids guesswork

1 · Name the practical question

Separate the situation from assumptions. Do you need moving logistics, a school transition, a health coverage update, or continuity of specialized family support?

2 · Find the right office

Use a verified installation, government or plan source. A friend’s experience can be useful context but is not your official policy.

3 · Prepare one clear question

Ask what needs doing before or after your move, what documents are actually required, and how to verify completion. Share sensitive records only through the office’s appropriate secure process.

4 · Check the handoff

Confirm who owns the next step and how to follow up. If the move or situation changes, recheck the instructions; do not assume an old checklist still fits.

STOP or escalate: A family needing urgent medical care should use appropriate emergency or clinical services, not this exercise. For disputed orders, coverage decisions, mandatory processes or accommodations, contact the responsible qualified office. This page never files or changes anything.

Four fictional PCS handoff situations

PCS means permanent change of station. Every model answer below works without JavaScript. These are learning examples, not instructions to share actual family documents here.

A · A move plan needs clarification

Invented situation: A family has received a new assignment and is uncertain how to coordinate household goods, move planning and the gaining installation. They have not confirmed transportation instructions.

WHY: Moving steps and available assistance depend on the actual assignment and location. HOW: Start with official relocation support and confirm the relevant transportation contact.

CHECK: Ask which moving tasks must be verified with the installation or service transportation office. COMMON MISTAKE: Booking or spending based on a social-media checklist alone. WHEN TO STOP: Do not infer reimbursement or entitlements; obtain official guidance.

Model handoff for A

Installation relocation assistance / Plan My Move. Use official relocation resources to organize questions and confirm the appropriate transportation office. Plan My Move offers a tailored task list, but never overrides orders or policy.

B · A student will change schools

Invented situation: A child will attend school near the next installation. The family is not sure how to start asking about records, placement and local enrollment.

WHY: School transfers can involve local requirements and different education programs. HOW: Ask the installation school liaison for school-transition guidance.

CHECK: Verify what documents the destination school requires, without posting school or child records to a public website. COMMON MISTAKE: Assuming the previous school’s rules apply everywhere. WHEN TO STOP: Do not promise admission, course credit or special education placement; use the appropriate professionals.

Model handoff for B

Installation school liaison. A school liaison can help a family find accurate school transition resources, contact the next school and ask about transferring records. This practice does not decide school eligibility.

C · Health coverage information may need updating

Invented situation: After a move, a TRICARE-eligible family notices that its contact address may be outdated. A member wonders whether plan choices or referrals could be affected.

WHY: Address and eligibility record accuracy matters for health plan communication and possible plan options. HOW: Use TRICARE’s official moving guidance and check the family information in DEERS through an approved channel.

CHECK: Ask the relevant TRICARE contractor or office which actions apply to the family's plan, location and effective dates. COMMON MISTAKE: Canceling current coverage before confirming the right transition. WHEN TO STOP: For urgent care, seek appropriate clinical help; never decide coverage from this fictional example.

Model handoff for C

TRICARE moving guidance and DEERS update. Check the official moving rules and verify address/plan information through approved channels. Coverage and enrollment consequences depend on the specific circumstances; no account update occurs here.

D · Specialized family support needs a warm handoff

Invented situation: A family already works with installation support for special educational or medical needs. The next location is unfamiliar, and they want to coordinate resources without publicly describing anyone’s condition.

WHY: Availability of specialized support can vary by location. HOW: Ask the installation’s Exceptional Family Member Program (EFMP) Family Support office about an appropriate handoff to the next duty station.

CHECK: Find the right official contact and secure way to coordinate services. COMMON MISTAKE: Assuming EFMP support directly provides medical treatment or guarantees placement. WHEN TO STOP: Medical, school-service and assignment decisions belong to qualified offices; disclose personal records only securely when needed.

Model handoff for D

EFMP Family Support office. Ask about non-clinical support, available resources and a possible warm handoff to the gaining installation. Enrollment, medical services and assignment decisions are separate official processes.

Practice: choose the best starting contact

Use only the invented scenarios above. Select the first information source that best fits each need, then read why. Every choice gets a reason, including incorrect answers.
