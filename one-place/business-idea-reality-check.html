Business Idea Reality Check

Before you spend money on an idea, estimate its basic costs and test what you still need to learn. This is a simple one-product or one-service monthly example, not a forecast, loan decision, or profit guarantee.

Important: This estimates money left after the costs you enter, before taxes and any costs you omit. Include labor and your own time when evaluating whether work is worthwhile. Startup expenses, equipment, permits, loan payments, returns, seasonal demand, and other costs may change the picture. Business rules differ by country and location. You do not need a name, bank account or other personal details.

Three useful questions before starting

1 · Who has the problem?

Describe a real customer need. Do not assume someone will pay just because you like the idea. Ask potential customers what they currently do, without collecting their private information.

2 · What does each sale really cost?

Per-sale cost changes with each order or job, such as materials and transaction fees. Monthly fixed cost continues even when you make no sales, such as software or workspace rent. Your work time also has value.

3 · What evidence will change your mind?

Compare real alternatives and prices, then test interest with a no-spend conversation or simple prototype where appropriate. Do not assume interest means a purchase.

Understanding break-even

The basic rule is monthly fixed costs ÷ (price per sale − variable cost per sale). Round the required number of sales up. If each sale contributes zero or less, more sales cannot cover positive fixed costs.

Try the one-month cost check

Start with your own rough estimates or choose a fictional example. Use the same currency for every money field; no exchange conversion is performed. Leave out customer names and business secrets.

Costs and likely monthly sales

Use nonnegative amounts with at most two decimal places (up to 10,000,000 per sale, or 100,000,000 in monthly fixed costs). Units must be a whole number from 0 to 1,000,000. One sale means one item or one service session.
