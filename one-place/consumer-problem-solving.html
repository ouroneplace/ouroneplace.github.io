Understand a problem. Prepare a fair response.

Learn with fictional consumer problems. Separate the facts from assumptions, organize evidence, identify the right kind of help, and prepare a respectful message or safety plan you can review. This page does not file complaints or ask for identification.

Important: These are U.S.-specific examples, not legal advice. Contracts, laws, complaint channels, filing deadlines and remedies vary across states, countries and products. Do not assume that a common-sense request is a legal entitlement. For an urgent notice, threatened eviction, frozen account, fraud or lawsuit, check the actual deadline immediately and contact the relevant provider or qualified local legal help.

The missing lamp

Fictional case: A sample store marked a desk lamp delivered, but the customer has not received it. The customer wants a clear answer without sharing payment records on an open website.

Why this matters: Start with the seller's official support channel, preserve what was promised, and describe a specific reasonable request. Refund or replacement rights can depend on location, payment method, seller policies and the transaction.

3. Understand who can and cannot help


Before an agency complaint
For an ordinary purchase, try the seller through verified customer service and preserve proof of what you sent. For financial products, some specialized time limits apply, so prompt contact with the provider may be important. Do not ignore a court notice or wait for a company if a legal deadline may expire.

FTC complaint letter guidance (external)


Government roles differ
State consumer agencies can provide complaint channels. The CFPB forwards eligible financial complaints to companies for response. The FTC uses fraud reports to spot patterns, but does not resolve individual reports. A government complaint does not guarantee a refund or stop a court deadline.

USAGov state consumer offices (external)


Legal aid, when needed
USAGov lists free and lower-cost legal aid, including resources for military families and people with disabilities. Local availability and qualification criteria vary. Use qualified help for court filings, jurisdiction-specific rights, threatened loss of housing or unresolved legal deadlines.

USAGov legal aid (external)


International visitors
These U.S. examples are not global rights or complaint systems. Find your country's consumer regulator, financial ombudsman or legal-aid authority using a verified government or nonprofit source. Cross-border purchases can involve more than one jurisdiction.
