Emergency Reconnection Practice

Make a practical plan for getting word to one another when communications fail or people are separated. Three original fictional situations teach backup channels, safe reunion arrangements, and inclusion of children, caregivers, disabled people and pets. Nothing personal is required.

Real emergency? Stop using this lesson. Follow current local emergency officials, shelter or evacuation instructions. If someone may be in immediate danger, contact local emergency responders (911 in the U.S.) and use their guidance. Do not travel into a restricted area, cross floodwater, delay evacuation, or attempt an unsafe transfer to reach a meeting point. This page cannot monitor people, locate missing loved ones, provide live travel routes, or contact responders.

These U.S.-focused practices are planning examples, not guaranteed communications, shelter or rescue arrangements. Mobile and internet service can fail. Always confirm school, care-setting, building, animal and accessible transport procedures with responsible local staff before relying on a plan. Keep real names, addresses, contact numbers and sensitive care details out of this page.

Use a communication backup without assuming delivery

Why this matters

Calls, power and data services may be disrupted during a disaster. A plan should have more than one way to send a short check-in and a way to know who has actually responded.

Fictional situation

A fictional household has agreed in advance to check in after severe weather. One person cannot complete a phone call, but the phone still has an intermittent signal.

How to prepare

  1. Before a disaster, agree on a brief check-in message and a verified out-of-area contact willing to help relay information.
  2. If a call does not connect, try a short text if service allows, and follow the already agreed backup method. Texts may work when calls do not, but delivery is never guaranteed.
  3. Do not assume silence means someone is safe or unsafe. Keep following official protective instructions, and seek professional emergency help for a real imminent danger.

Check the result

Does everyone know the agreed first channel, backup channel and what to do without confirmation?

Common mistake

Repeatedly calling is not a substitute for a shared backup. A queued text is not proof the other person received it.

Stop and get live help: A real crisis requires current official instructions. Never delay evacuation or immediate safety actions to complete a check-in exercise.

Read why each choice helps or harms (works without JavaScript)
  1. Send a short text if possible, follow the agreed backup, and do not mistake an unsent or unanswered message for confirmation. Using a prepared alternative and verifying receipt when possible is more reliable than assuming a single channel worked.
  2. Keep dialing one number and ignore all other previously agreed channels. Repeated calls may fail during congestion; agree on alternative contact methods before an emergency.
  3. Assume everyone is safe because the message appears in the sender's phone. A message shown as sent, queued or displayed does not necessarily confirm delivery, reading or safety.

Choose safe reunion arrangements before anyone is missing

Why this matters

People can be at different schools, workplaces, homes or care settings when an event begins. A meeting location is only useful if local conditions and official instructions make reaching it safe.

Fictional situation

In a fictional evacuation, two adults are at different locations. A familiar neighborhood meeting point is inside the closed evacuation area.

How to prepare

  1. In a calm period, agree on a nearby place for a small localized event and a second option outside the neighborhood; verify accessibility and whether each place is appropriate.
  2. Choose a trusted out-of-area contact who agrees to receive brief updates. Keep contact information available offline and share it only with the people who need it.
  3. If a location becomes unsafe or authorities restrict travel, do NOT go there. Follow evacuation or shelter orders and communicate a safer official alternative when possible.

Check the result

Can household members name the agreed out-of-area contact, and understand that safety overrides a meeting place?

Common mistake

Do not use a destination just because it appears on a printed plan. Real conditions, mobility access and local orders may invalidate it.

Stop and get live help: Never travel toward a hazardous, closed or flooded place to meet someone. Follow responders and local officials; ask for live help if a person is missing or in danger.

Read why each choice helps or harms (works without JavaScript)
  1. Travel there anyway so nobody has to change the plan. A preselected meeting point is not permission to cross closures or enter a hazardous area.
  2. Post everyone's full locations and personal details publicly to help coordinate. Publicly sharing sensitive household location information can create privacy and safety risks. Use trusted official or agreed contacts.
  3. Follow current authorities, use the out-of-area contact or agreed safe channel, and revise the meeting plan only if safe. Protective orders and actual safety outrank a rehearsed destination. Trusted communication can support a safer alternative.

Include children, caregivers, disability and pets without assumptions

Why this matters

People who rely on others for transport, communication or daily support may not be able to follow a one-size-fits-all reunion plan. Children and pets also require authorized, realistic arrangements.

Fictional situation

A fictional household includes a person who uses assistive communication, a child at school and a pet. Their first household plan mentions only adult phone numbers.

How to prepare

  1. Ask each person, where appropriate, what accessible alerts, communication methods or trusted support they want; do not assume a companion can speak for them.
  2. Confirm school or care-provider pickup and reunification policies, authorized adults and any actual support arrangements before an event. Make a separate realistic plan for pets.
  3. Keep a private paper contact card or accessible backup with only necessary details. Decide who may access it and when to check for changes; do not post identifiable details publicly.

Check the result

Does the plan cover trusted pickup, accessible contact and care responsibilities without guessing what an institution will do?

Common mistake

An unapproved pickup, unconfirmed caregiver, or improvised inaccessible travel plan may endanger the people it is meant to help.

Stop and get live help: Never attempt unsafe lifting, transfers, unauthorized pickup or entry into a restricted school or care setting. Contact relevant staff or emergency responders for real situations.

Read why each choice helps or harms (works without JavaScript)
  1. Treat the same adult-only phone list as sufficient for everyone. Different needs and institutional policies require an accessible, authorized contact and reunification arrangement.
  2. Confirm the person's preferred communication support, the school's authorized release process and a realistic pet backup. This respects consent, practical limits and official responsibilities without publishing private household information.
  3. Plan to collect the child from any door regardless of instructions. Unverified pickup or entry is unsafe; confirm authorized procedures directly with school or care staff.

Create a household discussion outline

Choose a planning concern, a backup method, and a safety check. The page creates generic preparation wording only. Discuss real contacts and locations privately with the people involved, on paper or through a trusted channel. This site does not accept, save or transmit those details.

Changes clear older output so a stale plan cannot be mistaken for a current one. Never publish private family details publicly, including where children are staying or a person's medical needs. Check details and permissions privately with trusted people and authorities.

Paper version (works without JavaScript)

  1. Agree on a first contact channel and at least one backup, including an out-of-area contact willing to help.
  2. Privately record one nearby and one out-of-neighborhood meeting option; do not use either if unsafe or contrary to orders.
  3. Confirm children, caregiver/communication access, pets and who is authorized to help.
  4. Review, share securely and practice a brief check-in during a calm period. Keep an offline copy only with people who need it.
