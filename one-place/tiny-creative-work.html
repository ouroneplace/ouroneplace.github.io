Make, Revise, Keep: A Tiny Creative Work

You do not need to be an artist or have a perfect idea. Make a small original piece, change one thing intentionally, then choose whether to keep it private.

Your choices stay yours. This page does not send, upload, post, permanently store or automatically save what you write. Avoid entering private names, addresses or sensitive stories. If you close or reload without making your own copy, the words may be lost.

The exercise is optional, self-paced, and does not grade creativity. You can use paper or simply read the example. Sharing with other people is always a separate choice.

Three moves: make, revise, keep

  1. 1. Notice a detail. Pick an ordinary moment: light on a window, a walk, a cup cooling. A specific detail gives you a place to begin.
  2. 2. Change or reframe it. Turn the detail into an image, action or comparison. Revise one word to make it closer to what you mean. There is no single correct result.
  3. 3. Keep a version. Read your piece once. Choose whether to print, make a local plain-text file, copy it onto paper or leave it private. A saved copy is not proof of a reliable backup until you can find and open it.

Try a paper-only example: Fill three short sentences: “I noticed ___.” “It reminded me of ___.” “I want to remember ___.” You can draw, write, dictate to a helper you trust, or skip any part.

Make your own three-line piece

Pick a small moment that does not identify anyone. Your words are not graded, scanned or shared. Each line can be short; try replacing one vague word with a more concrete detail during revision.

If a line feels too personal, change it or leave this page. Do not write about someone else's private experience without permission.

Choose what happens next

Review what you wrote before saving. You can change any field and review again. The preview becomes out of date after an edit, but your typed words stay in the fields.

Keeping vs sharing: A downloaded file stays on the device or location you choose, not on One Place. Check that it really opened. Make a second protected copy of anything important, and consider who can access it. Downloading is not publishing or sharing. Do not use a shared printer or device for anything private without considering who else might see it.

Copyright: In the U.S., original human-created expression fixed in a tangible form can qualify for protection without registration; short phrases and generic ideas alone may not. The legal outcome depends on the actual work and circumstances. This activity does not decide ownership, register copyright, or grant permission to use somebody else's writing or images.
