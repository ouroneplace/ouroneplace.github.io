01 · LIGHT & COLOR

What color can you make?

Move red, green and blue brightness sliders. Screens combine these colors of light. See whether you can make yellow, cyan, magenta, white—or a shade you invent.

Mix the light

Experiment with a starting mix:

SCREEN LIGHT SAMPLE
Yellow · RGB(255, 255, 0)

Try turning all three sliders to 255: the screen becomes white. This model illustrates how digital displays mix colored light—not how paint mixes. Your display affects the exact appearance.

02 · PATTERNS & DESIGN

Build a little symmetry.

Choose how often one shape repeats around a center. Notice how a small rule can create a rich pattern. No art skills required.

Design your pattern

What to notice: The same petal is copied and rotated around the center. Try an odd number, then an even number.

Six repeating petals · Ocean teal · 60° between petals

Each petal has the same size and shape. The number of petals changes the rotation between copies. Patterns in art and nature can have many forms of symmetry.

A tiny discovery can lead somewhere wonderful.

Color: Red and green light together can appear yellow; red and blue can appear magenta; green and blue can appear cyan. Add red, green and blue at full brightness on a screen and the result is white. Mixing paints is different.

Symmetry: Rotational symmetry means a shape or design can match itself after turning by a certain angle. Our flower patterns repeat an identical shape around one point.
