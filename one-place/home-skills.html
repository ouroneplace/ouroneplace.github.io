HOME SKILLS · LEARN, CARE, REPAIR

Know your home. Care for it with confidence.

From the kitchen and bathroom to laundry, heating, yard and emergency readiness: explore practical home skills. You don't need to know what to ask. Choose a room or search for a problem.

These 36 starting lessons are real, readable instructions—not promises of future services. They can't cover every home, model, climate, disability or local code. Always compare with your specific equipment instructions.

Outside resources are optional. Each background reference opens a separate browser tab and may have its own notices or commercial content. You can learn the basics on One Place without leaving; return to this tab to continue. Always check your particular appliance’s official manual.
Skills aren't the same as permission or safety. Homeowners can do many ordinary tasks themselves, but gas lines, live electrical wiring, structural damage, roof work, garage-door springs, major sewage or mold problems, and suspect lead/asbestos may require trained professionals. Renters may need landlord permission. Never attempt a dangerous repair just to avoid asking for help.

Start with a few things every household can learn

Know how to spot a water leak, test smoke and CO alarms, use an HVAC filter correctly, clean without mixing chemicals, and recognize an emergency. Then build up to simple repairs.

DIY doesn't mean going it alone. A wise homeowner knows the difference between routine care, a repair that requires extra caution, problems you should only inspect, and jobs that need a qualified specialist.

Start here: know your home and basic tools ＋

Before choosing a job: Find your equipment's brand and model number, read its official manual, identify hazards and permissions, and decide whether you can safely shut off the relevant supply. A general lesson does not override the model manual or local requirements.

  • Flashlight: See accessible leaks, labels and loose parts without reaching into equipment.
  • Camera and notebook: Record the model number, starting condition, and what changed. Avoid posting personal information.
  • Measuring tape: Check dimensions before obtaining replacement strips, filters or parts.
  • Cloth and soft brush: Clean compatible surfaces without abrasive damage; check the product label.
  • Correct screwdriver or hand tool: Use only on safe, owner-accessible parts; never open electrical or gas equipment.
  • Gloves and eye protection: Choose protection for the specific task; gloves do not prevent electrocution.
  • Manufacturer manual: Check the actual equipment's shutdown, replacement, cleaning, and reassembly procedure.

When a step is unclear, stop and consult the manual or a qualified person. There is no requirement to perform a repair yourself.

Clean kitchen surfacesKitchen · Daily or when soiledRoutine＋

What to have: A surface-appropriate cleaner, water, cloth

Try it safely, step by step

  1. Remove loose food and debris.
  2. Wash with suitable soap/detergent and water; follow the surface manufacturer’s instructions.
  3. Rinse if the product label calls for it and dry with a clean cloth.
  4. Disinfect only when needed, using a labeled product and its required contact time.

How to tell it worked: The surface is visibly clean and dry.

Stop and get help if: Never mix household cleaners or disinfectants; stop if a product is damaging the finish.

Check a toilet for a silent leakBathroom & Plumbing · At least annuallyRoutine＋

What to have: A few drops of food coloring

Try it safely, step by step

  1. Remove the tank lid carefully and set it somewhere secure.
  2. Place food coloring in the tank water, not the bowl.
  3. Wait about ten minutes without flushing.
  4. If dye appears in the bowl, suspect a leaking tank seal; flush immediately afterward.

How to tell it worked: You know whether the tank is losing water and whether repair is needed.

Stop and get help if: If the tank is cracked, overflowing, or the shutoff does not work, seek qualified help.

Recognize the hazard — do not attempt the repair

  1. Observe exterior signs of leaks, cracks, sagging or garage-door problems from the ground.
  2. Document symptoms without approaching unstable areas.
  3. Keep people away from damaged structures or malfunctioning doors.
