Four skills to use in a real job search

1 · Choose a direction

Start with a kind of work, training goal or workplace need. You can explore without committing to a career change.

2 · Show evidence of a skill

A skill sounds stronger when you can describe a real situation, something you did, and what happened next. Personal projects and caregiving can provide transferable examples when described truthfully.

3 · Prepare one answer

Use a simple structure: situation → your action → result or what you learned. Avoid claiming numbers or success that you cannot verify.

4 · Check a lead safely

Verify jobs through an employer's known website. Be cautious if a recruiter demands fees, bank information or a deposit before a genuine hiring process. Learn what to verify.

Verify a job lead before sharing sensitive information


  1. Find the employer's real website or independently known contact information. Confirm the opening exists and duties, work location, pay structure and hiring steps are understandable.
    2. 

  2. Be wary of surprise texts, rushed offers, upfront equipment or training fees, demands to send money back from a check, or requests for banking/identity details before legitimate onboarding.
    3. 

  3. Ask what application format, interview access or accessibility support you need. In the U.S., EEOC guidance explains disability accommodation in hiring; other countries have different rules.
    4. 

  4. If a recruiter seems suspicious, stop sharing information and use independently verified employer contacts. If money was sent, contact the payment provider promptly and consult official reporting channels.


A professional-looking website or interview is not proof an offer is genuine. This is a general checklist, not a guarantee a particular job is safe or available.
